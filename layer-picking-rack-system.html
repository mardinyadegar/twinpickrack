Layer Picking Rack System

Floor-level pallet flow lanes built for layer picking—operators clamp full layers of cases and set them on the outbound pallet in a single move.

Storage Systems

Why Choose a Layer Picking Rack System

Picking less-than-full-pallet orders by hand is slow: a picker travels to the slot, moves cases one at a time onto the outbound pallet, then travels to the next location. A layer picking system replaces that case-by-case handling with a layer clamp attachment that lifts one or more complete layers at once and sets them on the pallet being shipped.

The system is built from two- or three-deep, floor-mounted pallet flow lanes. Separators between the lanes give the layer pick attachment the clearance it needs to reach in and clamp, while gravity flow keeps the next pallet presented at the pick face. Where manual picking typically moves 100 to 250 cases per hour, a layer picking operation can process more than 1,000.

How It Works

From Full Pallet to Outbound Load, a Layer at a Time

1

Replenish

Full pallets load into the back of two- or three-deep floor-level flow lanes, keeping replenishment traffic out of the pick aisle.

2

Present

Gravity flow feeds the next pallet forward to the pick face, while lane separators keep clearance open for the clamp attachment.

3

Clamp & Build

The operator clamps one or more layers and sets them on the outbound pallet, building the shipment layer by layer instead of case by case.

Where It Fits Best

Is a Layer Picking Rack System Right for Your Facility?

Best fit: Fast-moving SKUs picked in less-than-full-pallet quantities.

Layer picking is most common in beverage and consumer goods distribution, in areas with heavy volumes of partial-pallet picks. Usually only the top-moving SKUs are good candidates—packaging has to be robust enough to clamp, and pick volumes have to justify moving product a full layer at a time. We'll review your inventory and order profile to confirm which SKUs qualify before a layout is locked in.

Key Advantages

  • Over 1,000 cases per hour versus 100 to 250 when picking by hand
  • Two- or three-deep floor-mounted pallet flow lanes
  • Lane separators provide clearance for the layer pick attachment
  • Gravity-fed FIFO rotation with replenishment kept out of the pick aisle
  • Fewer touches per case, cutting picker travel and manual lifting
